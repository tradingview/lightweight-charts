## How it works

The chart starts with only enough data to fill the screen and loads older data as you scroll back, the way a chart backed by a server would.

1. The number of bars that fit on screen is the time scale's [`width`](/docs/api/interfaces/ITimeScaleApi#width) divided by its `barSpacing`. The chart loads that many bars plus 20%. See [Number of visible bars](/docs/time-scale#number-of-visible-bars).
2. [`subscribeVisibleLogicalRangeChange`](/docs/api/interfaces/ITimeScaleApi#subscribevisiblelogicalrangechange) reports each scroll. When fewer than 10 bars are left to the left of the screen, the handler asks the data feed for more.
3. The data feed returns the older bars together with the ones already loaded, and [`setData`](/docs/api/interfaces/ISeriesApi#setdata) replaces the series data. A short delay stands in for a network request.

`Datafeed` only makes sample data. Replace `getBars` with a request to your server.
